# Recovering farm ground after war damage

These steps are for planning only. **Do not enter damaged ground** if there may be unexploded ordnance, chemical containers, buried services, fire-damaged structures, or unstable soil. Keep people and livestock away until the local mine-action authority, fire service, environmental regulator, and a qualified soil professional say the area is safe.

The sources below describe procedures. They do **not** publish a defensible price for most war-damage work. Therefore, a dollar amount is not shown unless a source gives an exact rate and the calculation can be reproduced. A hectare is **2.471 acres**, but that conversion is not used where the source does not publish an acre-based price.

## Crater

1. **Keep out and mark the crater.** Fence it from a safe distance, take photographs without entering, and ask a qualified mine-action or explosives team to inspect it. The [International Mine Action Standards land-release guidance](https://www.mineactionstandards.org/standards/) supports the clearance and land-release procedure, but it does not publish a farm-crater inspection price.
2. **Test the spoil and nearby soil before moving it.** First separate basic soil-fertility testing (pH, nutrients, and salts) from specialized contamination testing. The [University of Minnesota Farm/Field soil-testing instructions](https://soiltest.cfans.umn.edu/testing-services/farmhorticultural-field) describe ordinary agricultural soil sampling and fertility testing. They do not establish prices for petroleum, metals, explosive residues, or firefighting chemicals/PFAS.
3. **Separate suspect soil from clean soil.** Do not spread spoil until the regulator or soil professional approves its use. Put hazardous material on a licensed waste route.
4. **Repair the hole only after clearance and test results.** Use approved clean fill, place it in thin layers, and compact each layer so the field does not sink later. Get separate written quotes for excavation, hauling, clean fill, and compaction.
5. **Replace topsoil and control erosion.** Put verified clean topsoil back on top, shape the surface so water drains safely, seed a suitable cover, and check the area after heavy rain. The [USDA NRCS Conservation Practice Standards](https://www.nrcs.usda.gov/resources/guides-and-instructions/conservation-practice-standards) explain how to find the applicable local technical standard; they do not publish a universal price.

## Burn

1. **Wait for the fire service to say the area is safe.** Keep away from hot spots, weak structures, gas cylinders, ammunition, ash, and damaged electrical equipment. The [USDA Forest Service fire guidance](https://www.fs.usda.gov/managing-land/fire) explains fire-safety and response context, but it does not publish a farm inspection price.
2. **Map the burned area and keep ash and debris contained.** Keep ash, treated timber, plastics, batteries, fuel containers, and metal out of feed, water, and cropland. Use the local regulator's instructions for hazardous waste.
3. **Test ash, soil, water, feed, or crops as directed.** Treat basic soil-fertility testing (pH, nutrients, and salts) as a different service from petroleum, metals, explosive residues, or firefighting chemical/PFAS testing. The [University of Minnesota Farm/Field soil-testing instructions](https://soiltest.cfans.umn.edu/testing-services/farmhorticultural-field) support ordinary agricultural soil sampling, but do not establish prices for those specialized analyses.
4. **Stabilize bare ground before the next storm.** Use the erosion-control practice selected by the local soil professional, such as a suitable cover or surface protection. The [USDA NRCS Conservation Practice Standards](https://www.nrcs.usda.gov/resources/guides-and-instructions/conservation-practice-standards) describe how local standards are selected; they do not publish a universal per-hectare price.
5. **Return animals and crops only after results are acceptable.** Keep animals off until feed, water, and soil results meet the regulator's requirements. Do not add lime, compost, or other amendments unless a soil professional recommends them.

## Trench

1. **Fence the trench and keep machinery away from the edge.** Ask a qualified person to check for collapse, buried cables, pipes, and unexploded ordnance. The [IMAS land-release guidance](https://www.mineactionstandards.org/standards/) supports the mine-action and clearance context, but it does not publish a trench-survey price.
2. **Control water before filling.** Divert clean water away from the trench and repair blocked outlets so the trench does not become an erosion channel. Use the applicable local [USDA NRCS Conservation Practice Standard](https://www.nrcs.usda.gov/resources/guides-and-instructions/conservation-practice-standards).
3. **Separate rubbish and suspect material.** Keep metal, treated timber, fuel containers, and contaminated soil separate from clean earth. Use licensed transport and disposal where required.
4. **Fill in thin, compacted layers.** Use approved clean material, compact each layer, and do not bury organic debris that will later settle.
5. **Replace topsoil and seed a cover.** Leave the repaired surface level with the field, seed a suitable cover, and inspect it after rain.

## Contamination

1. **Isolate the area.** Fence and label it, stop food, feed, and water use, and prevent dust and runoff. Do not taste, burn, bury, or move suspect material.
2. **Identify the contaminant and map its boundary.** Use an environmental professional to choose samples from source material, soil, water, feed, and produce as needed. Keep basic soil-fertility testing (pH, nutrients, and salts) separate from petroleum, metals, explosive-residue, and firefighting chemical/PFAS testing. The [University of Minnesota Farm/Field testing instructions](https://soiltest.cfans.umn.edu/testing-services/farmhorticultural-field) describe ordinary agricultural soil sampling, but specialist chemical analysis must be arranged with a qualified laboratory.
3. **Choose a remedy with the regulator.** Depending on the contaminant, depth, soil volume, and disposal classification, the remedy could be excavation and disposal, clean cover, treatment, monitored recovery, or continued restriction. The [EPA Superfund cleanup process](https://www.epa.gov/superfund/superfund-cleanup-process) describes remedy investigation and selection; it does not publish a generic agricultural remediation price.
4. **Carry out only the approved remedy.** Keep clean and dirty equipment separate, track each load, and use licensed transport and disposal where required. Do not combine excavation, capping, treatment, hauling, or hazardous-waste disposal into one unsupported number.
5. **Verify cleanup before farming.** Resample and follow the regulator's release criteria before returning crops, animals, irrigation water, or workers to the area. Keep the results and restrictions with the farm records.

### Cost-record rule

Before adding a dollar figure, record the exact published rate, its original unit (for example, dollars per sample or dollars per acre), the affected quantity, and the calculation. If the source does not publish that rate, use **“Cost: Local quote required — no defensible published per-hectare rate was identified.”** Do not turn a general machinery, labor, or technical-standard source into a total war-damage remediation estimate.
